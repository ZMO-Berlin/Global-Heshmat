About this project

Global Heshmat – Following the Egyptian Sculptor Hassan Heshmat around the world.
Artworks in public spaces.

Heshmat was born in a small town on the Nile and began working with clay as a child. At the age of twelve, his father allowed him to choose his own career, and Heshmat went to Cairo to attend the School of Applied Arts. There he meets a teacher who would later also become famous, the sculptor Saeed Sadr.

In 1952, the monarchy in Egypt was abolished. The military coup by the Free Officers against the British-backed monarchy gave rise to a national project that many Egyptians had desired since the beginning of the 20th century. The Nasserist state was much more in tune with Heshmat’s understanding of art, and this state was indeed interested in his art about the so-called little people. It fit in well with the socialist state’s aspirations.

Heshmat was already a well-established artist, when in 1957 he went to Bavaria (Germany) with a scholarship of the DAAD in order to study the manufacturing of porcelain in the city of Selb. Unlike clay, porcelain allowed him to cast multiple small artworks that were affordable to the middle class – an aim he had formulated early in his life and an understanding of “Art for All” or “Kunst für alle”, as historically associated with the German art and social reform movements around the turn of the 20th century.

Throughout his life, Heshmat was supported by one person in particular: Tharwat Okasha, a polyglot genius who served as Minister of Culture several times under Nasser. After Heshmat returned from Selb, Okasha asked him to design a gift for Egypt’s state guests. Heshmat interprets an unfinished head of Queen Nefertiti in brown quartz, which is on display in the Egyptian Museum in Cairo. From 1958 to 1988, this reproduction of the ancient bust in porcelain was presented to 82 heads of state.

Heshmat was an internationally renowned artist who found admirers all over the world. His sculptures can be found not only on the Nile, but have also been exported to Brazzaville, Frankfurt am Main, Warsaw, Malmö, East Berlin and Boston. In 10th of Ramadan City, the founder of a well-known fashion company, the Copt Louis Bishara, had a small open-air museum for Heshmat built in a public park. Various industrialists have erected Heshmat’s art on their premises. A large relief by him still adorns the officers’ club on Zamalek’s main street.

Heshmat held solo exhibitions all over Europe, among other in Paris and Budapest in 1957, in Susteren in 1968, 1970 in Bonn and Radhus, 1978 in Roskilde or 1979 in Ystad. In 2000 Heshmat was awarded the State Appreciation Award, Egypt’s highest award.
