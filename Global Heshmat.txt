Intro
Heshmat was born in a small town on the Nile and began working with clay as a child. At the age of twelve, his father allowed him to choose his own career, and Heshmat went to Cairo to attend the School of Applied Arts. There he meets a teacher who would later also become famous, the sculptor Saeed Sadr.
In 1952, the monarchy in Egypt was abolished. The military coup by the Free Officers against the British-backed monarchy gave rise to a national project that many Egyptians had desired since the beginning of the 20th century. The Nasserist state was much more in tune with Heshmat’s understanding of art, and this state was indeed interested in his art about the so-called little people. It fit in well with the socialist state's aspirations.
Heshmat was already a well-established artist, when in 1957 he went to Bavaria (Germany) with a scholarship of the DAAD in order to study the manufacturing of porcelain in the city of Selb. Unlike clay, porcelain allowed him to cast multiple small artworks that were affordable to the middle class - an aim he had formulated early in his life and an understanding of “Art for All” or "Kunst für alle", as historically associated with the German art and social reform movements around the turn of the 20th century.
Throughout his life, Heshmat was supported by one person in particular: Tharwat Okasha, a polyglot genius who served as Minister of Culture several times under Nasser. After Heshmat returned from Selb, Okasha asked him to design a gift for Egypt's state guests. Heshmat interprets an unfinished head of Queen Nefertiti in brown quartz, which is on display in the Egyptian Museum in Cairo. From 1958 to 1988, this reproduction of the ancient bust in porcelain was presented to 82 heads of state.
Heshmat was an internationally renowned artist who found admirers all over the world. His sculptures can be found not only on the Nile, but have also been exported to Brazzaville, Frankfurt am Main, Warsaw, Malmö, East Berlin and Boston. In 10th of Ramadan City, the founder of a well-known fashion company, the Copt Louis Bishara, had a small open-air museum for Heshmat built in a public park. Various industrialists have erected Heshmat's art on their premises. A large relief by him still adorns the officers' club on Zamalek's main street.
Heshmat held solo exhibitions all over Europe, among other in Paris and Budapest in 1957, in Susteren in 1968, 1970 in Bonn and Radhus, 1978 in Roskilde or 1979 in Ystad. In 2000 Heshmat was awarded the State Appreciation Award, Egypt's highest award. You can get more information at the open access publication (Arabic/German): https://repositorium.zmo.de/receive/zmo_mods_00002340

-	The Hassan Heshmat Museum, 48J9+69R, Ein Shams Al Gharbeyah, El Matareya, Cairo Governorate 4533483. // The Museum of the artist Hassan Heshmat in Ain Shams is one of the precious house museums in Egypt. Its former owner, the sculptor Hassan Heshmat (1920-2006) worked with numerous materials like clay, wood, metal, stone or porcelain in sizes between 10cm and 10m. His works can be found all over Egypt. He donated his private house, workshop, gallery and sculpture garden to the Ministry of Culture which opened the museum in 2018, https://www.fineart.gov.eg/Eng/musem/Musem.asp?IDs=15 .

-	Metro Station Ain Shams, Ain Shams Sharkeya, Ain Shams, Cairo Governorate 4630001. // The Metro Station hosts the ‘Statue of Love’.


-	The Dawn of Egypt, 7QVJ+XGG, 10th of Ramadan City 1, Al-Sharqia Governorate 7065843. // The sculpture The Dawn of Egypt, eight-metre high and made of white stone, was erected in 1982 at Midan Gala’a (https://www.google.com/maps?q=30.0952756,31.3427727&entry=gps&shh=CAE&lucs=,94259549,94297699,94284469,94231188,94280568,47071704,94218641,94282134,94286869,100796593&g_ep=CAISEjI1LjQ5LjkuODM4ODk5MTgzMBgAIIgnKlssOTQyNTk1NDksOTQyOTc2OTksOTQyODQ0NjksOTQyMzExODgsOTQyODA1NjgsNDcwNzE3MDQsOTQyMTg2NDEsOTQyODIxMzQsOTQyODY4NjksMTAwNzk2NTkzQgJJTg%3D%3D&skid=676269cd-6338-4e61-98be-8ad9f83825d2&g_st=im) . Every day, tens of thousands of people passed by the eight-metre-high statue. Popularly known as Gala'a Masr, as it stood near the bridge of the same name in el-Nozha and referred to the Anglo-Egyptian Agreement of 1954 on the evacuation of British forces from the Suez Zone. In 2022, it had to make way for road construction in Cairo. By courtesy of Marie L. Bishara (see also > BTM) it now stands completely renovated at this roundabout in 10th of Ramadan City.

-	BTM Factory entrance, Statue, 10th of Ramadan City,: Misr & El Sudan St, 10th of Ramadan City 1, Al-Sharqia Governorate 7065846. // In 1960, Louis Bishara founded a textile printing mill, which he turned into Egypt’s first private-sector apparel factory in 1980, BTM (since 2024 Bishara Apparel Group). Louis Bishara became an admirer of Heshmat’s work and assembled a private collection. In addition, he posited statues on his factory premise in 10th of Ramadan City. This work of art stands in the entrance to his factory: ‘Mother and Child‘. (Foto: E. Gothe 2025). His eldest daughter Marie is a designer as well as vice chairwoman of the Bishara Group. She continues the sponsorship of artworks by Hassan Heshmat.

-	BTM Factory premises, Statue, 10th of Ramadan City,: 7QWH+HXH 10th of Ramadan City 1. // In 1993, Bishara commissioned Heshmat to design a figure for his fashion factory in 10th of Ramadan City on the occasion of the opening of a tailored suit factory. Here too, Heshmat worked with the motif of women and men from different perspectives merged in one figure, but focuses on the modern fashion aspect, thus creating a fairly atypical work out of basalt stone with a mosaic. 

-	Oriental Weavers, 10th of Ramadan City: 7P5X+8XM, Ismailia Desert Rd, El Sharqeya, 10th of Ramadan City 1, Al-Sharqia Governorate 7067171. [Obelisk & Löwe, photo see autobiography] With the gradual privatisation of Gamal Abdel Nasser's socialist planned economy under his successor Anwar Sadat, Heshmat established close relationships with industrialists such as Mohamed Farid Khamis (1940-2020), founder of the carpet manufacturer Oriental Weavers, and Louis Bishara, founder of the fashion company BTM. The Oriental Weavers Group was founded in 1979 by industrialist and entrepreneur Khamis. The Group has emerged from a single loom operation to the largest producer of machine-woven carpets in the world. According to Heshmat’s autobiography, a lion and an obelisk are standing at the headquarters in 10th of Ramadan City. The business tycoon, twenty years younger than Heshmat, became a cornerstone of Sadat’s infitah-policies (economic opening towards capitalism). Khamis served as head of the Egyptian Investors Federation.
The artist writes: “The obelisk represents the body of Egypt, with its name written in hieroglyphics and its elements connected at the top. Approximately 4 metres of basalt stone in front of the Oriental Weavers Factory in 10th of Ramadan City in 1994.” (1997: 118)

-	Open Air Museum and Gallery, Kafraoui Park, 10th of Ramadan City, 7QF3+7QW, 10th of Ramadan City 1, Al-Sharqia Governorate 7063150. // Louis Bishara donated a sculpture park for the industrial city with twelve of Heshmat’s works and a small gallery in the public Kafraoui Park, named after Hassaballah Al-Kafraoui (1930-2021), housing minister from 1977 to 1993. Under his auspices fell the first generation of new cities around Cairo, like 15th of May City or 10th of Ramadan City itself. 

-	El Gezirah Sheraton, heute: Sofitel Cairo Nilke El Gezirah, https://maps.app.goo.gl/C6RijpA4tvcH3bKc6 // Heshmat equipped various branches of the Sheraton in Cairo with towering statues. At the Gezirah Sheraton it was the statue of ‘Love’. This figurine was also commissioned as a gift edition in 13cm for visitors of the hotel.

-	Heliopolis Sheraton, today: Hilton Cairo Heliopolis, https://maps.app.goo.gl/va9vGo3drBXBF61k6 // In 1980, the three-metre high statue ‘Welcome’ made from granite stone was placed at the entrance of the Heliopolis Sheraton. The Sheraton chain in Egypt had close relations with the artist, as Eugen Auer, General Manager of the Heliopolis Sheraton from 1983-85 relays. He and his wife met the couple Hassan Heshmat and Madiha el-Gaky on various occasions. Ten years later a fire spread in the hotel, killing 16 people and leaving the 630-bed hotel in ruins. The hotel was closed and only reopened seven years later. Though the statue is shown in the artist’s autobiography after it had been cleaned of traces of the fire, it is not known, where the sculpture stands today.

-	Daughter of the Nile, Jollyville Mövenpick hotel, today the Steigenberger Pyramids Cairo, X4VF+9W:, Made of stone, approximately three metres tall. In February 1986, the Jollyville Mövenpick Hotel was set on fire during a major uprising by Egyptian security forces after rumours of a pay cut and extended service period. Further copies are today in the Hassan Heshmat Museum as well as in the public Kafraoui park. [Siehe Buch Eugen Auer]

-	Armed Forces Officers’ Club, Mohammed Mazhar, Zamalek, Giza Governorate 4271030. // The Armed Forces Officers’ Club in Zamalek (26 July Street) was established in the mid-1950s following the 1952 Egyptian Revolution as part of the expansion of military social institutions in Cairo. It offers member of the military recreation on Cairo’s most popular island. The facade relief has been renovated recently. Not fitting in style, right above it, is a second frieze, by Fathy Mahmoud (1918-1982), one of his main competitors at the time.

-	Agiba Petroleum Company, factory premises, Nasr City, https://maps.app.goo.gl/D9wkbs825716UQoJ7 // 4.5 metres made of stone and yellow and red brass. This calligraphy from 1995 with the name of the petroleum company Agiba was the last work of the artist according to his autobiography published in 1997.

-	Le Méridien Heliopolis, 51 El-Orouba, Almazah, Heliopolis, Cairo Governorate 4461030. // Le Méridien Heliopolis hosted a Horus figure, but the hotel is closed permanently today. Ca. 2 metres high, made from basalt stone. Foto from his autobiography 1997. 

-	Panorama October, Nasr City; 1979:. Al Estad, Qesm Than Madinet Nasr, Gouvernement Al-Qahira 4436011. // Made out of metal with a size of ca. two metres, the statue ‘Crossing the impossible – the Bar Lev Line and the Suez Canal’ was erected in front of the Panorama October in 1979. It symbolizes the surprise victory of the Egyptian forces against the Israeli army at the beginning of the 1973 war, when crossing the strategically important Suez Canal. Today the statue is missing; only a small maquette exists.

-	St. Therese Armenian Catholic Church, Heliopolis, 38RM+XR Heliopolis. The monument honours the inventor of the Armenian alphabet. The work is dedicated to Mesrop Mashtots and Isaac Partev, the creators of the Armenian alphabet, both of whom are considered saints in the Armenian Apostolic and Roman Catholic Churches. In the centre, the artist has engraved the following phrase from the Old Testament: ‘They serve wisdom and education and help to understand astute speech.’


Belgien:
-	Heilig Kruiskerk, Hasselt, Kruisherenlaan 29, 3500 Hasselt, Belgien, W8CG+9F Hasselt, Belgien. // In memory of 23 Belgian missionaries murdered in Buta (Congo) in 1965, Hassan Heshmat was commissioned in 1970 by the Order of the Holy Cross to erect a memorial in Hasselt, Belgium. It is still unknown whether he won a competition or how the artist from Cairo was chosen to execute the project? He produced the ceramics in his workshop in Cairo and flew them in to Belgium. The memorial still stands today, however, the ceramic panels depicting the missionaries’ robes, are no longer there. But the priest's heads, carved in bas-relief, are still in place. 
KADOC, the interfaculty Documentation and Research Centre on Religion, Culture and Society at the KU Lueven, founded in 1976, has incorporated research results from Hegasy’s projects into their archive and database.

Deutschland
-	Porzellanikon, Selb, 54Q8+PQ Selb. Rosenthal bought three designs from Hassan Heshmat. Only two have so far been found at the Porzellanikon in Selb: Woman with bowl (Model number 5123) and Standing Woman (Model number 5124). Both: Collection RAS, Permanent Loan, Oberfrankenstiftung Bayreuth. The artist himself calls these two figures in his autobiography: female farmer with basket and delicate farmer (“zarte Bäuerin” arab.?). He writes, that he exhibited the two figures (in their original form, i.e. not reworked by Rosenthal) 1968 in Rome and Geneva.

-	Mosaic outside private house, Ellwangen, house of Dr. Karl Kraus [Foto muss sicherlich noch bearbeitet werden.] According to his CV, Dr Karl Kraus worked in the pharmaceutical industry and had been a member of the Rotary Club (like Hassan Heshmat) since May 1969, initially in Egypt (Giza and Heliopolis), later in Brazil and Italy. In 1992/93, he became vice-president of the Rotary Club in Ellwangen. On the wall of his house is a work of art by Hassan Heshmat dated 1969. After Dr. Kraus' death, the owners of the house changed in 2025, but were surprised to be able to identify the artist thanks to the Berlin exhibition by Divan and ZMO in the same year.
Polen
-	12 enamelled clay works, Muzeum Narodowe w Warszawie, 62JF+RM Warschau. 
Research by curator Katharina M. Raab found that the Muzeum Narodowe w Warszawie houses a total of 12 enamelled clay works by Hassan Heshmat: one tile, two bowls and nine figurative works. The works have been part of the collection since 1957 and were created before Heshmats' decisive period in Selb. 
We like to thank curator Kaja Muszynska for her careful examination. (Email exchange between Katharina M. Raab and Kaja Muszynska on 4 June 2025)
